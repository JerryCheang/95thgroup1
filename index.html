Rebuilding Block D - Sustainable School Campus Vision

A Vertical Sustainable Campus for the Future

St. Joseph Diocesan College (The Fifth School)

Members: Barry Bunyamin, Michael Chau, Jayden Chou, Benson Vong, Kobe Wong

Executive Summary

To celebrate the 95th Anniversary of St. Joseph Diocesan College the Fifth School, our group proposes a new design for Block D, the heart of a sustainable, inclusive, and future-ready campus. The original Block D once housed classrooms, a music room, and teachers' lounges, but was demolished for legal reasons.

Our redesigned Block D will not only restore these facilities but also integrate green architecture and smart technologies to meet the evolving needs of both students and the T'oi San community. With renewable energy systems, natural ventilation, and community-oriented research spaces, the new Block D embodies sustainability, innovation, and unity, shaping a modern learning environment aligned with the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

Design Concept & SDG Integration

Design Concept

The new Block D is envisioned as a "Vertical Sustainable Campus", harmonizing modern education with environmental care. The six floors are arranged to maximize functionality, learning diversity, and community integration:

Floor Function
6/F Rooftop Garden, mini planting zone, solar panels, and "CDSJ5" shaped bushes.
5/F Diverse Industries Development Research Center (Chinese medicine, finance, and exhibitions).
4/F High School Classrooms (Chinese and English), Grand Hall, and discussion rooms.
3/F High School Classrooms (Chinese and English), Grand Hall, and collaboration rooms.
2/F Integrated Learning Complex (STEM, Arts, Music, Lab, IT) with discussion rooms.
1/F Indoor Sports Court.
G/F Kindergarten Playground.

Sustainable Features

Feature Description Benefit
Solar Energy System Solar panels with automatic sun-tracking installed on the roof. Reduces energy costs and emissions.
Rainwater Harvesting Collects and stores rainwater for irrigation and cleaning. Conserves water resources.
Natural Ventilation Vertical openings and crossflow windows improve passive airflow. Minimizes air-conditioning use.
Green Walls and Roof Garden Vertical gardens and rooftop plants integrated into the structure. Enhances biodiversity and thermal insulation.
Smart Energy Monitoring IoT sensors track energy usage and optimize building systems. Encourages data-driven sustainability learning.

Data & Survey Findings

Research shows a clear global shift toward energy-efficient and low-emission buildings, especially in schools. These findings support Block D's strategy: solar energy, rainwater harvesting, passive ventilation, and rooftop greenery.

Global Green Building Trends

Over 60% of new buildings include at least one sustainability feature.

Reference: Zuo and Zhao (2014); Zhang et al. (2019)

Environmental and Economic Findings

Schools using renewable energy and passive design reduced operating costs by about 25-30% over ten years.

Reference: Schneider-Marin et al. (2022)

Local Climate and Energy Context

Shading and solar reflection design can lower cooling energy demand by up to 22%, relevant for Macao's climate.

Reference: Bhagat and Kaushik (2025)

Integrated Sustainable Design Principles

Natural lighting and cross-ventilation can reduce electricity use and improve learning well-being.

Reference: Journal of Civil Engineering and Management (2014)

Interpretation: Block D can reduce emissions, lower long-term costs, and improve learning quality at the same time.

Other Suggestions

Blocks A, B, C, E

Add more greenery and sustainable features to improve biodiversity, air quality, and comfort.

Block C Interior Upgrade

Renovate G/F and 1/F to reduce narrow, pressured spaces and support inclusive learning (SDG 4).

Campus Color Redesign

Use neutral, low-reflective tones instead of strong blue to reduce potential bird-collision risks.
